This year, the agent carried most of our regulated volume, a few thousand cases a month, quietly. We built something that works, and it worked so consistently that we mostly stopped looking closely at it. That's the real story of this year: not that it broke, but that it got trusted before it was fully watched. Most of what it closed, we couldn't have explained after the fact if a client had asked.

That's what's changing, starting this week.

Each of you now owns one part of the system end to end, not just the cases that land on your desk, but its backlog, whether its decisions are written down, and sign-off on anything that changes how it works. `alert_triage` rotates across all of us given its volume; the other three each have a name on them starting today.

Mornings change too. Instead of launching runs and seeing what surfaces, we check four numbers for our own queue: how many risk cases are in it, how old is the backlog, what's stuck longer than it should be, and how many outcomes actually have a reason attached. We already found five places where cases were quietly getting stuck — one for over a month, with nobody able to see it. That's not a one-off fix, it's the first proof that watching these numbers directly, instead of waiting for a client to tell us, is worth doing every day.

None of this changes your judgment calls, those stay yours, same as always. What changes is that we'll know what's ours to watch, and we'll know before a client has to tell us something's wrong.

Good to have you all starting this quarter with me
